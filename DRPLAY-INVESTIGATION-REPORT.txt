0. READ THIS FIRST — THE HEADLINE FINDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The premise of this investigation ("consider adding Google Drive streaming for
MKV/MP4") is HALF WRONG, and this is the single most important thing in the
report:

  *** Google Drive byte-range streaming INTO mpv ALREADY EXISTS AND WORKS. ***

It is not something to be added. It is the app's existing, production-proven
playback data plane:

    mpv  --(HTTP GET, Range:)-->  http://127.0.0.1:{port}/stream/{fileId}
                                        |  (Rust `stream_proxy`, hyper server)
                                        v
                          Google Drive  files/{id}?alt=media
                          + Authorization: Bearer <access token>

Evidence that this is real and has been running in production:
  * Source: src-tauri/src/stream_proxy/mod.rs (891 lines) + server.rs (1097)
  * App log shows it bound on 33 separate sessions, e.g.
        [stream-proxy] started on 127.0.0.1:9295
  * mpv.log shows real playback through it, including a real byte-range seek:
        Run command: loadfile ... url="http://127.0.0.1:9295/stream/1h7OBAR3_..."
        [stream_callback] Opening http://127.0.0.1:9295/stream/1h7OBAR3_...
        [curl] proto=http ok=1 code=206 ...            (206 = Partial Content)
        [curl] stream level seek from 29593711 to 228638
        [ad] Opening decoder flac
        AO: [wasapi] 48000Hz stereo 2ch float

So the question is NOT "can we stream MKV/MP4 from Drive?" The answer is: the
transport is already correct and proven. The REAL question is "what is in the
way of VIDEO?" — and the answer is a short, concrete list of blockers, given in
section 7 and section 5.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
App maturity: a working, carefully built audio-only Drive music player. The
streaming core is stronger and more battle-tested than the README suggests.
The player surface is genuinely audio-only — video is not partially working,
it is structurally absent at three independent layers.

Requirement status (detail + evidence in section 4):
    PASS      8
    PARTIAL   3
    FAIL      1
    MISSING   2
    UNCERTAIN 4

Most important conclusions:
 1. Drive Range-streaming -> mpv: PASS, proven in production logs.
 2. Video: MISSING — blocked by `--no-video` in the mpv flag set, an
    audio-only extension allowlist in the Drive query layer, and zero video
    surface in the UI.
 3. mpv binary itself is fully capable of MKV/MP4 + hardware video decode
    (verified by running the shipped mpv.exe). The engine is not the blocker.
 4. The Rust proxy's audio-tuned timeouts (20s body-idle abort, 15s header
    timeout) are sized for audio. At video bitrates (2-20 Mbps) a slow Drive
    moment can exceed them and kill a video stream that an audio stream would
    survive. This is the #1 non-obvious risk.
 5. `track.streamUrl` and the service-worker lane are dead weight for playback
    and are a documented-but-unimplemented ADR. There is a real token-rotation
    race risk across three token owners (documented, not yet observed in prod).

5. "LOOKS IMPLEMENTED" ITEMS THAT ARE NOT WHAT THEY SEEM (honest flags)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are not broken features, but they are places where the code/README could
mislead you into over-estimating readiness for video:

(a) README line 27 says "Stream directly from Drive ... through an in-app
    service worker (/drive-stream/)". Reality (per docs/adr/
    ADR-legacy-drive-stream-lane.md AND my mpv.log evidence): playback goes
    through the RUST stream_proxy, NOT the service worker. The service-worker
    lane only serves metadata/cover range reads and a best-effort whole-file
    next-track prefetch. Two lanes, documented, but the README conflates them.
    -> Do not design the video feature on the assumption the SW lane carries
       playback. It does not.

(b) Both ADRs are "Proposed (chưa triển khai)" — i.e. NOT implemented:
      - ADR-legacy-drive-stream-lane: clarifies the 2-lane boundary + that
        `track.streamUrl` is a resume-marker, not a playback input.
      - ADR-token-single-owner: proposes Rust becoming the sole token minter.
        Reality today: THREE token holders exist (frontend tokenRefresh.ts,
        Rust stream_proxy cache, service worker module var) and TWO writers of
        the same OS keyring entry (frontend on rotation, Rust proxy on
        rotation). The ADR itself flags a cross-process rotation race as
        "structurally possible, no field incident yet".
    -> If video makes playback the dominant Drive consumer, this token race
       becomes higher-traffic and therefore higher-risk. See section 8.

(c) The Rust proxy's audio-tuned timeouts will be wrong for video:
      - UPSTREAM_BODY_IDLE_TIMEOUT = 20s (aborts if upstream goes quiet)
      - UPSTREAM_HEADERS_TIMEOUT  = 15s
      These were chosen deliberately for audio (a 20s gap is fine between
      compressed-audio chunks; the comment even says the audio buffer keeps
      playing through it). For a 20 Mbps video the demuxer wants data
      continuously; a 20s upstream stall mid-GOP will abort the response and
      surface as a stream error. Not a crash, but it turns a recoverable
      network hiccup into a hard stream failure. This needs a per-kind
      (audio/video) timeout policy, or a much more generous video idle window.

(d) Buffer flags are audio-sized:
      - --cache-secs=30, --demuxer-max-bytes=64MiB,
        --demuxer-readahead-secs=30, --demuxer-max-back-bytes=8MiB
      Fine for FLAC/MP3. For 1080p video the 8MiB back-buffer and 64MiB
      forward window are small relative to how much a single GOP can need;
      startup and seek may stutter more than a real video player would. Not a
      correctness blocker (my probe played fine), but a tuning task.

Coupling points that matter for adding video:
 1. mpv flag set is constructed once in mpv/process.rs (mpv_flags). `--no-video`
    is a startup flag, so enabling video is a process-lifecycle change, not a
    per-track option. (Good news: mpv accepts `video=yes` as a runtime property,
    so it could also be toggled without respawn — a design choice.)
 2. Frontend owns the allowlist (audioQuery.ts) that decides what Drive
    content is even modeled. Video needs that decision changed at the query /
    sync layer, which is upstream of the player — i.e. a data-model change.
 3. The stream proxy is media-agnostic (it just forwards Range + Bearer). It
    does NOT need to know it is serving video. This is the single strongest
    architectural fact for your plan: the proxy is already the right shape.
 4. There is no video surface (no <video>, no canvas VO host, no video route).
    The UI is built around an mpv sidecar that owns decode out-of-process, so
    where a video surface would live is an open architectural decision.

7. GOOGLE DRIVE STREAMING — READINESS, POINT BY POINT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Verdict: FEASIBLE WITH CHANGES — but you are NOT "adding streaming"; you are
"adding video on top of existing, working streaming."

1. Tauri HTTP/Rust proxy for Drive stream?  ALREADY EXISTS. stream_proxy is a
   hyper server on 127.0.0.1, per-session ephemeral port, already forwards
   Range and passes 206/Content-Range back. No new proxy needed.
2. Frontend can request a stream URL from backend?  YES — invoke
   `stream_proxy_start` returns the port; frontend builds
   http://127.0.0.1:{port}/stream/{fileId}. Used today by mpvAudio.ts.
3. What input does mpv take?  An HTTP(S) URL (it opens it with its curl
   client). It ALSO could take a path/pipe, but the app deliberately uses HTTP
   through the proxy (that's how auth is attached).
4. HTTP + Range support in the current integration?  YES, and proven. My probe
   and your own mpv.log both show `code=206` and real range-driven seeking.
5. CSP/Tauri security issues for mpv hitting localhost?  NO for mpv, because
   mpv is a SEPARATE PROCESS and is not subject to the WebView CSP. The CSP
   (`connect-src`/`media-src`) only governs the frontend's own fetch/audio
   elements. Caveat: if a future video surface were implemented as an in-app
   `<video>` element fetching the proxy, THAT would hit the release CSP
   (media-src does NOT include http://127.0.0.1 in production csp). Designing
   video to render in mpv's own output window sidesteps this entirely.
6. Auth token / Authorization header?  Handled in Rust: the proxy attaches
   `Bearer <token>` server-side; mpv never sees a token. mpv only hits localhost.
   Same mechanism works for video unchanged.
7. OAuth/token in Rust or frontend?  TODAY: split/duplicated (ADR-token-single-
   owner, not implemented). For video you do NOT need to move this now, but see
   risk R-T in section 8 — the duplicated ownership is a latent bug class and
   video increases Drive traffic through it.
8. Need a local range proxy, or pass Drive URL straight to mpv?  PROXY (already
   there). You cannot hand mpv a raw googleapis.com URL with the Drive OAuth
   scope without exposing the token to the engine; the proxy is the correct
   design and already exists.
9. If using the proxy, are Range/Content-Length/Content-Range/Accept-Ranges/
   Content-Type/206 correct?  Correct today, by code contract and by my live
   test (206 + Content-Range + Content-Length + Accept-Ranges all present;
   Content-Type mirrored from Drive). The proxy is content-agnostic, so video
   content-types pass through the same way.
10. Seek on large MKV/MP4 feasible?  YES, proven. mpv reported seekable=1 and
    issued targeted ranges on --start=90. Backward seek re-requests ranges
    (demuxer-max-back-bytes=8MiB bounds RAM, not feasibility).
11. Cache needed?  The SW lane has a byte-cache for metadata only; mpv has its
    own 30s cache. For video you likely do NOT want the SW byte-cache on the
    playback path at all — mpv's own cache is the right layer (that is exactly
    the ADR's "2-lane" conclusion).
12. Where should cache live?  Keep playback caching in mpv (its --cache /
    demuxer windows). Do not route video through the SW byte-cache; it is tuned
    for small metadata range reads.
13. RAM growth risk while streaming?  Bounded by mpv flags (64MiB forward,
    8MiB back). For audio this is generous; for video these are the numbers you
    will want to raise/tune. Proxy itself streams (no full buffering).
14. Can a long stream block a Tauri command?  No — streaming happens in the
    proxy's async server tasks and mpv process, not inside an invoke call.
    invoke("stream_proxy_start") only returns the port. The one blocking call
    is login_google_native (spawn_blocking), which is a startup-time action.
15. Can a streaming video hang a track switch?  A stalled upstream stream is
    aborted by the 20s idle timeout (turns into an error, not a hang) — but see
    5(c): that abort is aggressive for video. Also, changing tracks issues a
    fresh loadfile; mpv drops the old connection.
16. Cleanup if the user closes the app mid-stream?  YES for the normal path:
    mpv sidecar is pinned to a Windows Job Object with KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE, and
    kill_on_drop(true) is set. I verified no orphan mpv survived app exit. The
    proxy's async tasks die with the process. (Note: on a HARD kill the OS tears
    everything down; no leak observed.)
17. Google Drive API quota impact?  Real and worth planning for. Current design
    already spends quota: each play/seek range-read hits Drive, and the SW
    next-track prefetch downloads a WHOLE file (best-effort, unmeasured — ADR
    flags this as possible waste). Video amplifies this because video bitrate
    is ~100-1000x audio, so a 2-hour movie could mean a very large number of
    range requests / many GB per watch. Mitigations: (a) lean on mpv's cache so
    a single watch doesn't re-fetch, (b) measure/limit or disable the
    whole-file SW prefetch, (c) consider caching video chunks on disk.
18. Shared Drive / shared files / resource keys?  NOT HANDLED anywhere I can
    see. The proxy builds URLs as `files/{fileId}?alt=media` with no
    `resourceKey` support (relevant to link-shared large files). If your Drive
    movie library uses link-sharing or Shared Drives, this is a real gap to
    close before promising it works. (Adjacent: the app uses the broad `drive`
    scope, so My Drive/Shared Drive visibility is a permissions question, not a
    code gap — but per-file resourceKey is not implemented.)
19. Non-faststart MP4 (moov at end) over HTTP Range?  MP4 needs the `moov` atom
    to start. Over Range you can still seek to fetch a tail moov, but worst
    case (no faststart, moov far in the file) can force fetching large portions
    before playback starts. My fragmented-MP4 probe started fast (0.04s); I did
    not construct a worst-case tail-moov MP4, so treat the pathological case
    as UNCERTAIN — but it is a known class of issue. MKV does not have this
    problem (Cues are seekable via range; my MKV seek to 90s was instant).
20. MKV special handling?  No special handling needed for basic playback —
    proven (h264+aac MKV opened, seeked, played over Range). Practical notes:
    MKV can hold many codecs/subs the WebView2 could never decode, but because
    decode happens in mpv (not WebView2) that limitation disappears; that is
    actually an advantage of the existing sidecar architecture.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. BIGGEST RISKS (ranked, with why)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CRITICAL
 R1  Video is not a plugin; it is a data-model + process change. The allowlist
     (audioQuery.ts) that populates the library only matches 7 audio extensions,
     so MKV/MP4 never become Tracks. Until that changes there is nothing to
     play. Blocking, but small and local.

 HIGH
 R2  Audio-tuned proxy timeouts vs video bitrate. 20s idle abort + 15s header
     timeout were chosen for audio (see the code comment). On a 20 Mbps video a
     normal Drive hiccup can trip the abort and hard-fail the stream. Needs a
     video-aware timeout/cache policy.
 R3  Drive quota + cost at video bitrates. Every seek/underrun is a range read;
     the existing whole-file SW prefetch is already an unmeasured quota
     liability and video multiplies it. A single long movie watch can be many GB
     of Drive traffic. Must be measured/planned, not assumed.
 R4  Duplicate token ownership (documented race). Two processes can rotate the
     same keyring refresh token (frontend + Rust proxy); ADR says "structurally
     possible, no incident yet". More playback traffic through both = more
     exposure. Video raises stakes.

 MEDIUM
 R5  Buffer/decoder tuning for video (64MiB/8MiB windows, no HW-accel config
     in flags). Playable but may stutter; needs tuning per bitrate.
 R6  No video surface / architectural decision unmade. Where does the picture
     appear? mpv's own output window (no WebView changes, sidesteps CSP) vs an
     in-app surface (bigger change, hits production CSP for 127.0.0.1). Decide
     before writing code.
 R7  Shared-Drive / resourceKey support absent for link-shared files.

 LOW
 R8  README overstates the SW lane as the streaming path; two ADR boundaries are
     still "Proposed, not implemented" — documentation drift only, but it can
     mislead planning.

9. RECOMMENDED IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY (what to keep / add / never mix)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KEEP AS-IS (do not touch):
  - The Rust stream_proxy. It is media-agnostic, already Range-correct, already
    auth-correct, already proven. Video rides on it unchanged.
  - mpv sidecar + Job Object lifecycle + named-pipe IPC. Already leak-free.
  - mpv receiving an authenticated localhost URL (token never exposed to mpv).
  - The 2-lane boundary ADR (SW = metadata/download; proxy = playback).

ADD (new, isolated):
  - A video-capable mpv mode (start without --no-video, or toggle the `video`
    property at runtime) — decide spawn-time vs runtime based on the surface
    decision (R6).
  - A separate "video files" query/allowlist alongside (NOT replacing) the audio
    one, so the existing audio library and playback path are unaffected.
  - Video-aware proxy timeouts / mpv cache sizing.
  - (Optional, likely necessary) resourceKey handling for link-shared files.

DO NOT MIX INTO EXISTING PLAYER LOGIC:
  - Do not route video through the service-worker byte-cache — that cache is
    for small metadata range reads and is sized/tuned differently.
  - Do not add a second Drive token owner while a token owner is already
    duplicated; consolidate (ADR) if you touch this area.
  - Do not put a raw Google token in any URL or frontend state to "make video
    work quickly" — the whole proxy design exists to avoid that.

10. CONCRETE NEXT STEPS (safest order, based on what I actually verified)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. Decide the video surface (R6): mpv own output window vs in-app. This gates
    everything UI-side and avoids the production-CSP trap.
 2. Enable video in the mpv engine and make it runtime-toggleable if possible
    (avoids respawn churn); keep audio default unchanged.
 3. Add a video allowlist + Drive query for mkv/mp4 (keep audio path intact);

 4. Retune proxy timeouts + mpv cache/demuxer windows for video bitrates;

11. THE CLEAR CONCLUSION YOU ASKED FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS THE REAL STATE OF THE APP TODAY:
DrPlay is a mature, deliberately audio-only Google Drive music player. Its
streaming core is not missing — it is one of the stronger parts of the system:
a Rust localhost Range proxy feeds mpv over HTTP with 206/Content-Range, real
seeking, server-side auth, no token leak, and orphan-proof process cleanup.
The UI, the library model, and the mpv flag set are all intentionally
audio-only. I found no broken "looks-done" video feature to repair; the
video goal is a genuine new capability, sitting on top of a transport that
already works.

IF YOU ADD GOOGLE DRIVE VIDEO STREAMING NOW, THESE ARE THE THINGS THAT WILL
GET IN THE WAY (in order of likelihood):
 1. The library never lists MKV/MP4 (audio-only allowlist) — so "select a movie"
    cannot even happen. Fix this first; everything else is downstream of it.
 2. mpv is launched with --no-video — no picture appears no matter what URL you
    hand it (proven: 'video'='no' -> no video decoder).
 3. Video bitrates will trip the audio-sized 20s/15s proxy timeouts, turning
    recoverable network hiccups into hard stream failures.
 4. There is no video UI surface and no decision yet about where the picture
    renders (mpv window vs in-app; the latter is additionally blocked by the
    production CSP for 127.0.0.1).
 5. Drive quota/cost at video bitrates is unmeasured and the existing
    whole-file prefetch is already a suspected waste source.
 6. A documented-but-unimplemented token-ownership ADR leaves two writers of one
    keyring entry; higher traffic through both raises the latent race risk.

None of these are reasons NOT to proceed — the transport is genuinely ready and
the engine binary is video-capable. They are the concrete list of what "adding
video" actually entails, and the first two are small, local changes.
